## Judge criteria (in order)

1. A two-part confession: a To: line naming who it was meant for, and the
   message itself. Anything else loses to any two-part confession.
2. The reason it stayed in drafts is felt — too honest, too late, too kind
   or too afraid — and the words are specific and honest, not general.
3. The To: line and the message need each other; swapping the addressee
   would break it.
4. It reads as typed and never sent, not written to be sent: pure jokes,
   greetings and motivational lines lose.
5. The message is one line written exactly as it was typed, with no
   explanation after it. The reason it stayed in drafts should be felt in
   the words, not stated.

## Why it should win

The field will be full of generic apologies ("I miss you", "I never told
you I loved you") that fit any addressee and state their reason outright.
This one is specific (a job, bragged about), its addressee is load-bearing
("the job you keep bragging about" only fits a parent), and the reason it
stayed unsent is felt, never stated: "I'm sorry. I'm also relieved" holds
guilt and relief in one breath — the exact conflict that keeps a message in
drafts. It reads as the unsent text itself, not a description of one.
